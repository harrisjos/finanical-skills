Real World: The Plumber Who Owed $200k

A plumber switched to ABN and started invoicing $1,800 a week. He felt rich — new ute, holidays, tools on credit. He never once saved for tax. Four years later, the ATO caught up.

Result: $200,000 tax debt, plus interest and penalties. Lost his ute, his tools, and his house deposit. All because he didn't know the rules.

Facilitator Prompt: "Has anyone heard a story like this? What could he have done differently from day one?"
EMPLOYEE (TFN)
  • Pros: Sick leave, Annual Leave, Super paid for you. Tax deducted automatically.
  • Cons: Fixed wage. Harder to claim some deductions. Building someone else's dream.
CONTRACTOR (ABN)
  • Pros: Unlimited earning potential. Tax deductions. Freedom.
  • Cons: No sick pay. Must save own Tax (BAS). Must pay own Super.

GAME: "TAX DEDUCTIBLE — YES OR NO?"

Facilitator: Call out each item. Students write their guess (Yes / No) in the booklet first — then reveal the answer and reason.

# Item Answer Reason
1 Steel-cap work boots YES Protective PPE required for the job.
2 Work phone (used for calls & quoting) YES Tool of trade — must keep records of work use %.
3 Morning coffee on the way to site NO Personal expense. The ATO draws the line here.
4 Work Ute (used 80% for work — ABN) YES (80%) Pro-rata based on work use. Must keep a logbook.
5 Uniform / work clothes with logo YES Must be compulsory and occupation-specific.
6 Gym membership NO Personal health — not directly earning income.
7 Sunscreen (outdoor site work) YES ATO allows this for outdoor workers — keep receipts.
8 Power tools used on client jobs YES Tools of trade. Receipt = proof.
Key Message: "If you need it to earn your income and you wouldn't have bought it otherwise — it's probably deductible. When in doubt: keep the receipt and ask your accountant."
THE 30% RULE

"If you switch to ABN: Transfer 30% of every single payment into a separate 'Tax Account'. Do not touch it."

Business Structures: Sole Trader vs Company

Most students will start as a Sole Trader — but knowing where the road goes helps you plan.

SOLE TRADER (ABN)
  • Simplest & cheapest to set up
  • You and the business are the same legal entity
  • You keep all the profit
  • You are personally liable for any debts
  • Tax rate = your personal income tax rate

Best for: Getting started. Side hustles. Testing the market.

COMPANY (Pty Ltd)
  • Separate legal entity from you
  • Your personal assets are protected from business debts
  • Flat company tax rate: 25–30%
  • More admin & costs (~$800–$1,500/yr)
  • Looks more professional to clients and banks

Best for: Turning over $100k+. When you have employees. When you want asset protection.

Key Message: "Don't register anything until you've spoken to an accountant. A one-hour meeting could save you thousands. Most accountants offer a free first consultation."

The Hidden Cost of "Cashies"

Cash jobs feel like free money. But undeclared income creates hidden financial damage that compounds over years.

What You Think What Actually Happens
"I'm keeping all of it — no tax!" You're building zero financial track record. Banks can only lend on income they can see.
"It's just a bit of extra cash." ATO audit = back-taxes + interest + penalties. Can wipe out years of savings.
"No one will know." ATO data-matches bank deposits against your clients' tax deductions. More people get caught than you'd think.
"I'll sort it out later." Later = applying for a home loan and the bank only sees half your real income. Declined.
Facilitator Prompt: "The goal isn't to pay more tax — it's to have a financial record that proves you earn what you earn. That's how you get the loan. That's how you buy the house."
